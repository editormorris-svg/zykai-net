About ZYK

Build the institutional layer between schools and AI.

ZhiAn YunKe is the proposed China-focused education-AI company described in the consolidated business plan. Its thesis is that schools need more than access to powerful models: they need infrastructure, institutional knowledge, assessment, governance, training and accountable operations around those models.

Mission

Empower schools with secure, private and governable AI capabilities while keeping institutional control over data, access, knowledge and educational use.

Institutional control

Design around school identity, permissions, approved knowledge, policies and operational ownership rather than unmanaged individual accounts.

Compliance by design

Make governance requirements part of architecture and workflow design rather than documentation added after deployment.

Education expertise

Build around teaching, assessment, safeguarding, professional development and school operations.

Local capability

Develop school technical teams, local graduates and partner capacity instead of creating permanent dependence on one specialist.

Commercial clarity

Aim for understandable infrastructure, licence, hosting, support and training costs rather than uncontrolled per-user experimentation.

Open evolution

Allow approved local and open models to improve over time without forcing institutions to rebuild their entire AI environment.

How ZYK came together

Not a sudden pivot into AI — the convergence of a life in education.

ZYK brings together more than three decades of work across education, teaching, school leadership, curriculum and assessment, teacher development, international and multicultural learning environments, education ventures such as Oxbridge College, and increasingly deep work in digital systems, infrastructure and applied AI.

Education first

The starting point has always been schools, learners and teachers. Technology is useful only when it improves teaching, access, assessment, operations or institutional capability.

Many chapters, one direction

Earlier schools, colleges, programmes and professional roles are treated as building blocks rather than separate identities: each added practical knowledge of learners, staff, parents, systems, cultures and education markets.

International perspective

Work across different countries, cultures and education settings informs a design philosophy that must translate between local realities, international expectations and institutional constraints.

From classroom to infrastructure

Curriculum design, assessment, portals, local AI, servers, networking, governance and teacher training now meet in one architecture. ZYK is the point where those pieces become a coherent institutional platform.

References to earlier organisations, including Oxbridge College, describe founder experience and project history. They do not imply that those organisations are part of, owned by, or formally affiliated with ZYK unless separately stated.

Company thesis

ZYK is not intended to be an AI wrapper or a hardware reseller.

Infrastructure

The compute, storage, networking, model-serving and operational layer that makes shared institutional AI possible.

Education platform

User-facing tools plus Knowledge, Assess and Agent capabilities designed around school workflows.

Governance

Identity, permissions, audit, consent, retention, filtering, model policy and review through a common control layer.

Capability

Training for the people who teach with, govern, administer and support the environment.

Integration

LMS, SSO, APIs and school knowledge connect AI to institutional systems rather than leaving it as a separate website.

Lifecycle

Assessment, pilot, deployment, support, model evolution and eventual migration or expansion form one operational relationship.

How ZYK intends to work

Technology decisions should stay connected to educational and institutional responsibility.

Requirements before procurement

Begin with use cases, data, governance, users and operating constraints before deciding which models or hardware belong in the solution.

Pilot before scale

Validate a bounded set of workflows, measure adoption and operational burden, then expand only when evidence supports the next step.

Human accountability

AI can assist teaching, assessment and operations, but consequential academic, safeguarding and institutional decisions remain human responsibilities.

Model neutrality

Institutional identity, policy, knowledge and workflows should remain durable even as approved model providers and serving technology change.

Scale thesis

From one school to shared education infrastructure.

School

Prove useful workflows and governance inside one institution.

School group

Share selected infrastructure, policy and services while preserving campus-level boundaries.

Region

Explore common capacity, governance, model evaluation, training and assessment services for multiple schools.

Ecosystem

Develop universities, Graduate Academy, Host Schools and an evidence-based Innovation Lab around the operating platform.

Corporate structure, leadership titles, commercial targets and registration details remain planning matters until formally established. Those items are preserved in the project record and investor/planning materials rather than presented here as established company facts.
