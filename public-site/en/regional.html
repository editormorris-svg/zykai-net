Groups & regions

Standardise what should be shared. Preserve what should stay local.

ZYK's regional direction is designed for school groups and education bureaus that want common AI infrastructure, governance, knowledge and training without forcing every campus into an identical operating model.

Discuss a regional modelExplore platform architecture
The problem

AI adoption fragments quickly when every school solves it alone.

Different tools

Schools may independently adopt different public AI products, model versions, settings and data practices.

Different standards

Without a shared baseline, policies, content controls, consent and retention practices can diverge across campuses.

Duplicated cost

Infrastructure, procurement, integration and training may be purchased repeatedly rather than coordinated where sharing is appropriate.

Uneven capability

Schools with stronger technical teams move faster while others struggle to evaluate models, hardware and governance requirements.

Regional architecture

A shared control plane with local-school boundaries.

Shared AI capacity

Regional or group infrastructure could provide approved model serving and overflow capacity while schools retain defined local data and workload boundaries.

Common governance baseline

Leadership can define minimum policies for models, filtering, logging, consent, retention and approved use cases.

School-level overlays

Individual campuses can retain local curriculum resources, permissions, schedules and educational workflows within the common framework.

Central visibility

A future multi-school ZYK Control layer could provide group-level service health, policy status, audit visibility and utilisation dashboards.

Shared education services

Regional infrastructure should create educational value, not just centralise servers.

Shared ZYK Knowledge

Approved regional resources, curriculum guidance and common policies can coexist with campus-specific knowledge collections.

Regional ZYK Assess

Groups may standardise placement, diagnostic or progress-measurement frameworks while keeping teacher review and local reporting needs visible.

Common AI literacy

Teacher, leadership, IT, parent and student training can use a consistent regional framework with local delivery.

Model evaluation

A shared evaluation process can compare candidate models for language, curriculum, safety, latency and infrastructure cost before region-wide adoption.

Operating principle

Centralise governance where it reduces risk. Decentralise learning where local context matters.

Central

Approved model catalogue, baseline controls, infrastructure standards, common documentation, procurement guidance and regional support.

Local

School curriculum, class access, local resources, teacher workflows, campus-specific agents and appropriate data boundaries.

Portable

Schools should not be trapped by one model or one hardware generation. The architecture should permit controlled model and capacity changes over time.

This page describes a strategic regional architecture, not an existing education-bureau deployment or endorsement. Any public claim of a bureau, district or school-group partnership requires explicit evidence and permission.
